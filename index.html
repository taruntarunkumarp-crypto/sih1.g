Source-cited Ayurveda IPR & Regulatory Synthesis
🇮🇳 India Jurisdiction ● Confidence: Medium
Your formulation may require both intellectual-property and regulatory evaluation. Under Indian patent law and AYUSH regulations, herbal compositions face strict statutory exclusions that must be addressed through comparative synergy documentation and appropriate category licensing.
🛡️

Possible IP route

  • Patent eligibility should be evaluated based on novelty, inventive step and applicable exclusions.
  • Under Section 3(p) of the Indian Patents Act, inventions which are traditional knowledge or an aggregation of known properties are excluded from patentability.
  • To overcome Section 3(e) (mere admixture), proven experimental synergy data must demonstrate unexpected efficacy over individual herbs.
  • File a distinctive brand Trademark (Class 5) to protect your proprietary trade identity in the commercial market.
📋

Regulatory consideration

  • First determine whether the product is a classical medicine, proprietary medicine, food/nutraceutical, cosmetic or another category.
  • If classified as an Ayurvedic Proprietary Medicine, submit safety and efficacy evidence under Rule 158-B of Drugs and Cosmetics Rules to the State AYUSH Licensing Authority.
  • If positioning as a dietary wellness supplement, evaluate FSSAI's Food Safety and Standards (Ayurveda Aahar) Regulations, 2022.
🏛️ Traditional Knowledge & Prior Art
Herbal compositions with traditional Ayurvedic history are documented in ancient treatises (Charaka, Sushruta, Bhavaprakasha). TKDL examiners cite these texts against patent applications claiming known traditional therapies.
🌱 Biodiversity & ABS Compliance
Under Section 6 of the Biological Diversity Act 2002, mandatory prior approval (Form III) from the National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) must be obtained before applying for patents based on biological resources from India.
🎯

Recommended next steps

  1. Identify the product category (Classical, Proprietary, Ayurveda Aahar, or Cosmetic).
  2. Check existing traditional knowledge and prior art using TKDL and patent databases.
  3. Identify the appropriate IP protection (Synergy Patent, Trademark for brand, or Trade Secret for process).
  4. Check applicable regulatory requirements with State AYUSH Licensing Authority or FSSAI.
  5. Consult a qualified IP professional and AYUSH regulatory advisor when required.

Step 3: Is the formulation based on an existing classical Ayurvedic text?

Check against treatises listed in the First Schedule of the Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940.

📖 Classical Shastriya Formula
Exact recipe mentioned in authoritative classical texts (Charaka, Sushruta, Ashtanga Hridaya, AFI).
🔬 Modified Classical Composition
Based on classical herbs but incorporating modern dosage formats, excipients, or delivery vehicles.
💡 Novel Proprietary Combination
Innovative combination of herbs not explicitly paired in any single ancient treatise.
